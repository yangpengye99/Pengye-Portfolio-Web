Parallel Mailbox

Letters to a Parallel Universe

Post today away to another possible version of yourself — and receive one sentence that makes you want to keep going.

About

A short end-of-day ritual. No questionnaires, no uploads, no self-analysis — and explicitly not a personality test or fortune-telling. You pick photos that feel like today; a letter arrives from a version of you two to nine years down another life-line.

RoleSolo designer & developer
PlatformRED mini-tool · offline HTML5
DateAug 2026
Problem

Gentle encouragement, without writing a word

Students and early-career 18–34-year-olds scroll RED late at night. They want a moment to pause and some non-judging warmth — without journaling, filling in forms or exposing private data.

Zero input cost

Eight A/B photo choices replace typing. A nickname is optional and lives in memory only.

Personal, not predictive

The letter reflects today’s choices; it never claims to predict the future or diagnose you.

A reveal worth waiting for

In v1, opening the letter wasn’t surprising enough — v2 is built around that beat.

Narrative System

256 paths, fully deterministic

Choices resolve into one of four universes. Questions 1–4 score a carrier — people, place, object or ritual. Questions 5–8 score a direction — continuity, connection, guidance or possibility. Same choices, same letter, every time.

v2 · Blind-box envelopes

Narrativise the randomness

Two layers. The causal layer — universe, year, letter, photo — never changes. The surprise layer — envelope, wax, stationery and particles — is drawn by chance and explained as the physics of interdimensional mail, never as “more accurate”.

Iteration

From a vintage mailbox to deep space

v1 · Bone paper & a brass mailbox
  • Warm bone paper (#F4EFE4), archive red and a Song-style serif built a calm, archival mood.
  • A separate feedback page broke the flow between choosing and sending.
  • A 2.4 MB sending video and bitmap stationery pushed the package to 8.8 MB.
v2 · The night sky
  • Photos load into the envelope inline; you break the wax yourself on delivery.
  • Code-drawn warp starfield and CSS letter paper replace video and bitmaps.
  • Package shrank from 8.8 MB to 1.9 MB.

Interaction lab: three alternative mechanics

  • A

    Envelope collecting — each of the eight picks adds a visible layer to the envelope.

  • B

    Tonight in three frames — sort photos into keep, let go and carry to tomorrow.

  • C

    Two-way reply — three photo signals, then you choose how to answer.

Verdict: go for moderated research, no-go for production — the variants differ in several ways at once, so a preference couldn’t be traced to one mechanic. Lessons logged in the specs: animations must look right in their static end state, flight arcs must run horizontally, and a planet ring at 178% width was cut back to 142% so it stopped slicing the screen.

Visual Direction

Colour only where it means something

Text is black, white and grey only. Letter paper is always white (#FBFAF8, ink #2A2A2C). Colour comes solely from the photos, the envelopes and the wax — five tier sigils: postal bar, sprout, four-point star, constellation and compass.
